A short primer on Meta ads

How Meta ads work, in six cards.

Meta owns Instagram and Facebook. When we “run Meta ads”, we pay Meta to show our posts to people who don’t follow us.

1

An ad is a post we pay for.

It looks like a normal post, with a small “Sponsored” label. It shows up between posts from friends, in the feed, in Stories and in Reels.

Our 8 ads are 8 posts. Each one has a button that says “Learn more”.
2

We say who. Meta picks the exact people.

We tell Meta the rough group: people in India, aged 25 to 45. Meta decides which people inside that group see each ad. We don’t pick people one by one.

We keep the group wide on purpose. Meta finds the right people faster than we can guess them.
3

We set a daily limit. Meta never goes over it.

We tell Meta how much it can spend each day. It spends that money through the day and then stops. We pay for people seeing the ad, not for users.

Week 1: ₹3,600 a day. The whole plan can never go over ₹1,20,000.
4

Meta learns who says yes.

Each time someone taps “Start” on our page, Meta looks at who that person is. Then it shows the ad to more people like them. It needs about 50 of these a week to learn well.

This is why the first few days cost more. Meta is still learning.
5

The pixel is a counter on our page.

It is a small piece of code on the landing page. When someone taps “Start”, the pixel tells Meta. No pixel means Meta can’t learn, and we can’t see which ad works.

The pixel is part of the 3-day Meta setup.
6

We test, then keep the winners.

In week 1, all 8 ads run with small money. Then we switch off the ads that cost too much for each user. The money moves to the 2 or 3 ads that work best.

No one can predict the winner in advance. The test shows it.

Terms used in this plan

Campaign
The folder that holds the whole push. We have one.
Ad set
A group of ads inside the campaign, with its own daily budget. We have three, one for each ad direction in Part 1.
Ad
One post that people see. We have eight.
Landing page
The web page people reach after they tap an ad. It is not our website’s home page.
CPM
What it costs for 1,000 people to see an ad. In India, about ₹200.
Click rate
Out of 100 people who see the ad, how many tap it. About 1 is normal.
Cost per user
All the money we spent, divided by the users we got. This is the only number that decides which ad wins.
Learning
The first days of an ad, when Meta is still working out who to show it to. Changes during this time reset it.

Three parts. The ad, the page, the chat.

A person meets us three times. First in Instagram, where they see the ad. Then on our landing page, which they only reach by tapping the ad. Then in WhatsApp, where JARVIS talks to them.

What happens to 1,000 people who see an ad

People drop off at every step. That is normal. These are our guesses estimate. The first week of ads gives us the real numbers.

See the ad1,000 people
Tap the ad10
Tap “Start” on the page2 or 3
Send the WhatsApp message1 or 2
Answer JARVISabout 1

So: about 1 user for every 1,000 people who see an ad. At ₹200 per 1,000 views, one user costs about ₹200 to ₹240.

What counts as a user

Someone who sends the message AND answers JARVIS’s first question.

This is the number we count to 500.

Not a user

A tap, a page visit, or a “hi” with no reply.

We track these, but we don’t count them.

Why a new page, and not the website?

The website tells the whole OneStop story to people who come to read. Someone from an ad gives us 3 seconds. They need one line, one proof and one button. So this page has no menu and no links away. Every button opens WhatsApp.

What the button does

It opens WhatsApp with a message already typed: “Hi JARVIS 👋 I’d like to start.” The message carries a short code that says which ad the person came from. So we know which ad works, even when the pixel misses someone.

How we decide

Three rules. We agree on them before we spend.

Our target is ₹240 per user. An ad needs ₹3,000 of spend before we judge it. Below that, the numbers are too small to judge.

STOP

More than ₹480 per user

After ₹3,000 of spend, an ad that costs more than twice the target gets switched off. Same for an ad that fewer than 1 in 200 people tap.

WAIT

Between ₹240 and ₹480

Leave it alone for 2 more working days. Changing an ad resets Meta’s learning, and the cost goes up again.

GROW

₹240 per user or less

Give it 20% more money per day. Not more than that. Big jumps also reset Meta’s learning.

If Meta rejects a bold ad, we switch on its safer version the same day and appeal in parallel.
